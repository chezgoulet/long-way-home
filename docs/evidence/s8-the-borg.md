# Evidence: S8 — the Borg, first slice

Date: 2026-10-06. Gate S8 of `docs/ship-programme.md`. This slice is the rules, in the ship core,
evidenced by `tests/ship`. In the game it is reachable from the console (`ship borg <deck> <n>`).

## The rules

- **Drones convert the deck they hold.** `assimilated` rises with the number of drones not pinned
  by defenders — one drone alone takes an hour over a deck. Past half, the deck's systems are theirs
  outright: control is forced to nothing, no console reaches them and no counter-hack either.
- **They take the crew they find**, one each ten minutes, and each crew member taken is another
  drone. Those taken do not come back.
- **Driving them off does not undo it.** The deck stays Borg, and its systems stay lost, until
  engineers strip it: eight engineer-hours and twenty spare parts for a wholly assimilated deck, up
  to four engineers at once, and only after the drones are gone. Without parts it stays as it is.
- **Security pins them** as it pins any boarders; pinned drones convert nothing.

## What the tests establish

| property | test |
|---|---|
| Two drones on an undefended deck: assimilation under way within a quarter of an hour, crew taken, and the number of drones grown by exactly those taken | the Borg |
| Within the hour the deck is assimilated and its system is at zero control; a counter-hack does nothing | the Borg |
| Vented, the drones die and the deck is still Borg, its system still lost | the Borg |
| Sealed and stripped: engineers go to it (four at most), assimilation falls and parts are spent; a day later it is clean, the system answers again, and the parts spent equal the deck's share of twenty | the Borg |
| The assimilated are on no deck | the Borg |
| With no parts, a deck stays 80% Borg after a day | the Borg |
| With security aboard, two drones are cleared having converted almost nothing | the Borg |
| All of it survives a save | the Borg |

## Runtime asset replacement: the engine capability, assessed (2026-10-07)

The owner asked for the ship's assets to *turn Borg* and be stripped back. This is the first of the
two hard problems the full-scope mandate names, so it is its own assessment.

**What exists.** The engine can already repoint a texture at another shader at run time:
`gi.RemapShader` was routed through the SP bridge in `patches/0014` for the live panel work, and the
module calls it today (`module/ship/lwh_panel.cpp:193`, remapping three console textures to the
ship's status panel). With the module off the import slot is null and nothing changes. So the
*rendering* half of "turn Borg" — swap a section's shaders for Borg ones and swap back — is reachable.

**What is missing, and the blocker.** `RemapShader` remaps by *shader name*, globally: every surface
in the merged BSP using `textures/foo` changes at once. It cannot say "the surfaces of deck 9" or
"the systems in this compartment" — a brush face in a compiled BSP carries no entity, no deck, and no
section. Per-section replacement therefore needs one of: (a) authoring the published decks so each
deck's surfaces use deck-unique shader names (a large, per-deck texture remap through the stitcher,
and a rebake of the map and its visibility), or (b) an engine change that tags surfaces with a
section and filters the remap by it (a new renderer capability, its own patch, with a rebase cost).

**Measured, so the claim is honest.** The nearest thing that works today is a **per-deck shader
table for the decks we own**. The five generated decks (6, 7, 12, 13, 14) are authored by
`tools/shipmap/gendeck.py`, so their wall, floor and ceiling shaders are ours and deck-unique; a
Borg-tinted variant per shader (generated by script, like `panelart.py`) plus a `gi.RemapShader`
call when `deck.assimilated` crosses `ASSIMILATED` — and the reverse on strip — would turn those
decks Borg visibly and strip them back, with no engine change. The published decks cannot be done
this way without name collisions, which is why (a) or (b) is the real fix.

**Built and verified (2026-10-07, later the same day).** The nearest thing that works is now in, and
it works on the decks we own:

- The five **generated decks split their surfaces into four sections** (`tools/shipmap/gendeck.py`):
  west wall, east wall, ends and ceiling, and floor, named `textures/lwh/deck06wall0`, `..._wall1`,
  `..._wall2`, `..._floor`, declared in `tools/shipmap/data/lwh_borg.shader` and shipped in the pak.
  A section can be addressed by shader name on its own.
- A **Borg tint shader** `textures/lwh/borg` (the same image, a green `rgbGen const`) ships with it.
- The module's `BorgAssets` pass (`module/ship/g_ship.cpp`, called each frame) **turns a deck's
  sections in order as `deck.assimilated` rises to `ASSIMILATED`** — so the deck is taken *part by
  part*, not all at once — and remaps them back when it is stripped. It is gated by `g_ship` and
  inert with the module off.
- **Verified on the merged ship** (`scripts/borg-deck-check.sh`, `g_shipTest 25`): standing on a
  generated deck, half-assimilating it (some sections Borg), wholly assimilating it, and
  photographing the surfaces:

```
SHIP: borg deck test: standing on deck 6 at (-4512 -3584 -19312)
SHIP: deck 6 is turning Borg (2 of 4 sections)
SHIP: deck 6 has turned Borg (4 of 4 sections)
PASS  a deck the simulation assimilates turns Borg part by part in the world, and is stripped back
      screenshots: .../lwh_borg_deck_before.tga, .../lwh_borg_deck_partial.tga, .../lwh_borg_deck_after.tga
```

(The harness keeps a Borg party aboard while assimilating, so the ship's own damage control does not
strip the deck back as fast as it grows and the section count stands for the photograph.)

`scripts/borgfx-check.sh` also proves the rendering capability on a published deck directly (`ship
borgfx on|off` remaps a known surface live and back), so the ability does not depend on the generated
decks being present.

**What remains, and it is the honest boundary.** The **published decks** share shader names across
decks, so `RemapShader` still cannot address one of them alone; they need either the stitcher pass
that gives each deck unique shader names (option (a) above, a rebake) or the engine section tag
(option (b), its own patch). And the score is a screenshot: the tint is for a person to judge, the
swap is in the log.

## The rest of S8

- **To be seen.** The owner asked for the ship's assets to *turn Borg* and be stripped back. That
  is replacing a section's textures and models at run time by `assimilated`, which is an engine
  capability that does not exist yet. The number that would drive it does.

  crew do not yet appear as drones with their own faces.

  (above). The **published** decks are still one shader address short of per-section: they share
  shader names across decks (the blocker above), and their surfaces are not yet authored into named
  sections. The model itself is still per-deck (`Deck.assimilated`); sections are a presentation of
  that fraction, not independent simulation state.
